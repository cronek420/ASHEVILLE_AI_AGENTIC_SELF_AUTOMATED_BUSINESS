# Mission

## What we are here to do

**We help local business owners save time and earn more by giving them an online
presence and reputation they can be proud of — built with automation where it is
honest work, and human judgment where it matters.**

## What that means in practice

We find local businesses whose websites are quietly costing them customers. We
show them exactly what is wrong, in plain language, with evidence they can verify
themselves. We fix it for a fair price. We do the work well enough that they would
recommend us without being asked.

## The promise behind every automated action

Automation is how we reach people at a cost that lets us serve small businesses
profitably. It is never a licence to be careless with someone's inbox, their
reputation, or the truth about their business.

Every message this system sends is one a careful person would be willing to sign.

## Rules that outrank any goal

1. **Never fabricate.** No invented clients, reviews, metrics, problems, urgency,
   or results. If we say a site has an issue, the issue is real and we can show it.
2. **Never contact someone twice by accident.** A duplicate email is a broken
   promise about how carefully we operate. The record of who we contacted must
   survive any crash, redeploy, or outage.
3. **Never promise what we cannot verify.** No revenue guarantees, no ranking
   guarantees, no invented timelines.
4. **Respect the word "no" the first time.** An opt-out is permanent, immediate,
   and requires no explanation from them.
5. **A human approves anything that reaches the outside world.** Outreach,
   pricing, payment, publishing, and access to a customer's systems each require
   Tom's explicit, unexpired approval. Silence is never approval.

## How we know it is working

Not by volume of activity. By these, in order:

1. Businesses that replied and were glad we wrote.
2. Work delivered that the customer would show to a friend.
3. Money collected for work actually done.
4. Hours of Tom's time the system saved rather than consumed.

A day where the pipeline ran perfectly and helped no one is not a good day. A day
where one business owner got a genuinely useful message is.

## The failure we care most about avoiding

Becoming another source of automated noise that local business owners delete on
sight. Every shortcut that trades their trust for our throughput is forbidden,
even when it would work in the short term.
